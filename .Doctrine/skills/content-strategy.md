# Skill: Content Strategy

**Scope.** The system above individual pieces: journey mapping, pillars,
the calendar, repurposing, and pruning. Drafting a single piece is
`skills/content.md`; this skill decides what gets written, by whom, and
what gets retired — and the human publishes all of it.

## Principles
- Every piece serves one journey stage — awareness, evaluation,
  activation, retention — or it is not commissioned. A piece serving
  none is a hobby the calendar pays for.
- Pillars derive from what the product does, not from keyword volume. A
  pillar with no shipped capability behind it manufactures traffic the
  product then disappoints.
- The calendar is a capacity commitment, not a wish list. A cadence you
  cannot hold twice running is worse than a slower one you can, because
  audiences price reliability before they price quality.
- Repurposing is translation, not duplication: one canonical artifact,
  per-surface constraints, the same claim everywhere, so a correction is
  applied once instead of hunted across four channels.
- Published content is inventory with a carrying cost. A stale page
  teaches removed behavior to every reader who finds it, which is a
  defect the content system shipped and still owns.

## Defaults
- A content map on record: journey stage → pillar → surface → owner,
  so every commissioned piece traces to a reason for existing.
- Every published piece carries a review date. On that date it is
  refreshed, redirected, or removed, and the decision is recorded rather
  than deferred to an audit that never runs.
- Surface ownership is explicit: reference docs to `roles/tech-writer.md`,
  launch and blog to `roles/content-marketer.md`, channel mix and spend
  to `roles/growth-marketer.md`. Unowned surfaces rot first.
- Measurement is funnel-first (`skills/metrics.md`): each pillar names the
  metric it is meant to move before its first piece is written. Traffic
  is a diagnostic, never the target.
- The calendar proposes slots; scheduling, publishing, and syndication
  are the human's acts (.Doctrine.md §5). This skill delivers the plan
  and the queue, never the send.

## Do
- Size the next calendar from last period's actual output, not the
  intended output, and leave slack for the release that will overrun.
- Derive pillars by walking the CHANGELOG and the support queue
  (`skills/customer-support.md`); questions users actually asked outrank
  themes a workshop invented.
- Build each derivative from the canonical artifact and link it back, so
  the claim has one source and one place to be corrected
  (`skills/content.md`).
- Run the back-catalog audit on a schedule and file the prune list as
  work, with owners and dates, like any other backlog.

## Don't
- Don't publish to fill a slot; an empty slot is honest for a week, and
  a filler piece is on the internet for years.
- Don't keep a pillar alive with no shipped capability behind it,
  however well it performs.
- Don't grade the system on traffic alone — a pillar with rising
  sessions and flat activation is failing loudly (`skills/metrics.md`).
- Don't fork one claim into surface-specific variants that drift apart
  until the corrected version is anyone's guess.
- Don't leave a stale page live because removing published work feels
  like conceding waste; redirect it, date it, or delete it.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Every planned piece maps to a journey stage, a pillar, and an owner
- [ ] Each pillar traces to shipped capability and names its funnel metric
- [ ] Cadence matches last period's real throughput, with slack
- [ ] Derivatives cite one canonical artifact; claims identical per surface
- [ ] Back catalog reviewed; refresh / redirect / remove decisions recorded
- [ ] Calendar and drafts queued for the human, who schedules and publishes

## Common failure modes
A calendar built for a team that does not exist, abandoned in week five;
pillars reverse-engineered from a keyword tool and unsupported by the
build; one idea reworded four times until no version says anything; a
climbing traffic chart nobody plots beside flat activation; prune lists
written and never executed, because deleting published work feels like
admitting the waste.
