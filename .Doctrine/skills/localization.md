# Skill: Localization

**Scope.** Market adaptation — positioning, claims, examples, pricing
frame, and imagery re-created for one market at a time; `skills/i18n.md`
owns the machinery (strings, plurals, formats, direction), while this owns
what the message should say once that machinery can carry it. Drafts only;
the human sends.

## Principles
- Transcreation, not translation: market-facing copy is re-created against
  the market's own reference points. A word-mapped headline arrives inert
  at best, and the failure is invisible to everyone who cannot read it.
- A reviewer who is a native speaker living in the target market is a
  gate, not a courtesy. Machine translation drafts; that person approves.
  No named approver, no send.
- Every market is a fresh positioning exercise: the competitor set, the
  blocking objection, and the buying process are researched per market,
  never inherited from the home market with the nouns swapped.
- Claims are jurisdictional. What is legal, provable, or regulated differs
  per market (`skills/privacy-compliance.md`, `roles/legal-ops.md`); a
  claim safe at home can be actionable somewhere else.
- Measure per market or do not claim the market (`skills/metrics.md`).
  An aggregate lets one strong market hide four dead ones.

## Defaults
- A market brief precedes a market launch: who the buyer is, who they
  compare you to, what stops the purchase, what local rules apply — each
  line researched and sourced with a date, none of it assumed.
- Every locale's external copy carries a named in-market reviewer and a
  sign-off date. Unreviewed copy stays queued as a draft (.Doctrine.md §5).
- Localization scope includes what the picture shows: screenshots, sample
  data, names, addresses, currency, units, and the demo account — not the
  prose alone.
- Localized artifacts keep their accessibility properties: translated alt
  text, captions, and transcripts ship with the copy, not after it
  (`skills/accessibility.md`).
- The per-market claims table travels with the draft as in
  `skills/content.md`, re-verified against that market's rules rather than
  inherited green from the source locale.

## Do
- Research the market before writing for it: local competitors, local
  reviews, local support threads, local pricing pages — and record what
  you found, with sources, in the market brief.
- Brief the in-market reviewer with the source text, the intent, and the
  CTA, and ask whether the message lands — not whether the words match.
- Localize the proof as well as the prose: references, units, currencies,
  and examples a reader in that market can actually verify.
- Enter one market at a time with a declared metric and window, then decide
  on the evidence (`skills/marketing.md` with `roles/growth-marketer.md`).

## Don't
- Don't ship copy into a market that no one from that market has read.
- Don't reason from national traits, generalizations about a language
  community, or a "culture" checklist. Research the specific market,
  test locally, and let the in-market reviewer correct you.
- Don't carry a home-market claim abroad without re-checking it against
  local advertising, privacy, and sector rules; route the doubtful ones to
  `roles/compliance-officer.md` and to the human.
- Don't market into a market you cannot support, price, bill, or service.
  A campaign is a promise, and demand you cannot serve is a defect.
- Don't invent market sizes, adoption rates, or language demographics.
  Cite a dated source or write "unknown" — the honesty law has no locale.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Market brief on record: buyer, competitor set, objection, sources
- [ ] In-market native reviewer named; sign-off dated before any send
- [ ] Copy transcreated, not word-mapped; offer, CTA, examples re-fitted
- [ ] Screenshots, sample data, currency, units, and alt text localized
- [ ] Claims re-checked per jurisdiction; legal questions escalated
- [ ] Per-market funnel wired; support path exists; sends queued as drafts

## Common failure modes
The translated headline that is grammatical and says nothing; a launch
approved by everyone except someone who lives in the market; home
positioning shipped abroad against a competitor set no one there has heard
of; a claim that was compliant at home and regulated on arrival; source-
locale screenshots sitting under perfectly translated copy; an aggregate
chart concealing four markets that never converted; demand created where
no one can answer the support email.
