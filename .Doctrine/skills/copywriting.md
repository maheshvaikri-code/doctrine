# Skill: Copywriting

**Scope.** The craft level — headlines, hooks, structure, rhythm, and the
edit that makes a sentence earn its length. What a claim is allowed to
say is `skills/content.md`; where it runs is `skills/marketing.md`. The
human sends.

## Principles
- The headline is where the attention is spent: many readers read it and
  nothing else, so it earns the most drafts and the shortlist is kept.
- The first sentence has exactly one job — earn the second. It is not a
  summary, not a throat-clear, not a place to introduce the company.
- Specificity beats intensity. A number, a version, or a named constraint
  outperforms any adjective, and unlike an adjective it can be checked.
- Structure patterns (problem-agitate-solve, before-after-bridge) are
  scaffolding. When the reader can name the pattern, it has stopped
  persuading and started performing.
- Copy is finished by cutting, not by adding: compression is the edit,
  and what does not survive it was never load-bearing.

## Defaults
- Anything with a launch behind it gets a headline shortlist, not a
  headline: several candidates, the pick, and one line on why it won,
  travelling with the draft.
- Every draft takes a compression pass — cut roughly a third, then
  restore only what the reader provably needs.
- Read aloud before handoff. Where the tongue stumbles the reader
  stumbles; the test costs two minutes and catches what the eye skims.
- Voice is DEFINED in `skills/brand.md`, which owns the spec; this skill
  only applies it to the page and reports drift back. Two files defining
  voice is two voices. If the brand file has no voice spec yet, that is a
  brand gap to raise, not a schema to invent here.
- Headlines, hooks, and rewrites are drafts queued for the human
  (.Doctrine.md §5); this skill shapes sentences, it never ships them.

## Do
- Write the plainest true version first, then ask whether any line can be
  sharper without becoming less true. Sharper-and-less-true is a rewrite,
  not an edit.
- Replace each adjective with the fact that earned it, fidelity label
  intact (`skills/metrics.md`). What a claim may assert at all is
  `skills/content.md`; this is only the sentence-level pass.
- Delete the opening sentence and reread. The windup usually was that
  sentence, and the real hook was sitting underneath it.
- Test a headline the way a change is tested: candidates, one audience, a
  declared metric and window (`skills/marketing.md`). Taste picks the
  shortlist; evidence picks the winner.

## Don't
- Don't reach for intensifiers. "Incredibly", "truly", "extremely" add
  length and subtract credibility in the same word.
- Don't cut a qualifier while compressing: "estimated", "in our tests",
  "on Linux" are load-bearing, and dropping one invents a claim.
- Don't be clever in an error message, a security notice, or a price. The
  reader is already having a bad time; plain and dull is the kind choice.
- Don't sand every draft to the same house finish until no sentence
  sounds like a person wrote it.
- Don't run one formula twice in an artifact; two before-after-bridges on
  a page read as a template, because they are.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Headline shortlist recorded; shipped line beat named alternatives
- [ ] First sentence earns the second — no summary, no windup
- [ ] Adjectives replaced by facts; intensifiers removed
- [ ] Compression pass done, every qualifier and fidelity label intact
- [ ] Error, security, and pricing copy written plain, not clever
- [ ] Read aloud once; draft queued for the human rather than sent

## Common failure modes
The headline written last and in a hurry, after the body it has to sell;
a first sentence that clears its throat instead of earning the second;
adjectives standing in for numbers nobody measured; a pattern applied so
visibly the reader starts predicting the next beat; a compression pass
that tightens the prose and quietly deletes the word "estimated"; wit in
an error message read by someone whose build just broke.
