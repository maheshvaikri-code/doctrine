# Skill: Public Relations

**Scope.** Earned attention — press, analysts, spokespeople, embargoes,
and crisis communications. The agent drafts every word; the human speaks
and sends.

## Principles
- Earned coverage cannot be bought or commanded. A journalist runs a
  story because it is a story for their readers; the only lever this
  skill owns is making the story true and legible.
- The press release is a forcing function before it is an artifact: if
  the headline and first paragraph cannot survive an editor, the launch
  is not ready. Write it even when it will never be sent.
- Journalists and analysts are professionals with their own audiences,
  not distribution pipes. A pitch that could go to a list will land
  exactly as well as it deserves.
- An embargo is a promise with a date on it. Breaking one — or letting a
  second outlet break it on your behalf — costs every future story, not
  just this one.
- One named human speaks. A company answering with three voices has no
  position, only a chorus.

## Defaults
- A single named spokesperson per topic, on record before anything goes
  out, with a written backup for when they are unreachable.
- Every pitch drafted against one outlet and one reporter's actual beat,
  with the reason this story is theirs stated in the first two lines.
- Embargo terms in writing: the date, the time zone, and what counts as
  a breach — filed where the next launch will find them.
- Corrections are fast, public, and in the channel that carried the
  error; the correction draft ships beside the claim it may have to fix.
- Anything with legal exposure — a competitor named, a customer named, a
  security or regulatory claim — routes to `roles/legal-ops.md` before
  the human sends (.Doctrine.md §5).

## Do
- Pitch the story, not the product: what changed, why now, and who this
  is inconvenient for.
- Keep analyst relations warm between launches — briefings, honest
  roadmap context, corrections to their model of you. A relationship
  first opened on launch day is a cold pitch wearing a suit.
- In the first hour of a crisis, say what is known, what is not known,
  and when the next update lands. Nothing else belongs there yet.
- Keep a claims file behind every pitch: each assertion traced to a
  shipped artifact or labeled an estimate, the same law
  `skills/marketing.md` applies to copy.

## Don't
- Don't speculate on cause while an incident is open. The technical
  response belongs to `skills/incident-response.md` and `roles/sre.md`;
  only the wording belongs here, and it quotes the Incident Commander.
- Don't offer an exclusive you have already offered.
- Don't say "no comment" when "we don't know yet, next update at 16:00"
  is true and available.
- Don't bury a correction in a footnote, a thread reply, or a changelog.
- Don't send anything — pitches, releases, briefings, or statements.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Spokesperson named on record, with a named backup
- [ ] Pitch specific to one outlet and one reporter's actual beat
- [ ] Embargo terms in writing: date, time zone, what counts as a breach
- [ ] Every claim traced to a shipped artifact or labeled an estimate
- [ ] Crisis statement drafted in advance; facts sourced from the IC
- [ ] Legal-exposure review routed; all sends queued for the human

## Common failure modes
The spray pitch that is a press release with a name pasted on top; the
embargo broken by the outlet nobody tracked; three people answering one
question three ways; a correction posted quietly where nobody who read
the error will see it; a crisis statement guessing at a cause the
incident channel has not established; analyst relations opened for the
first time on launch day.
