# Skill: Demand Generation

**Scope.** The funnel machine — capture, nurture, landing pages,
attribution, and the handoff to sales. Not 1:1 selling; that is
`skills/sales.md`. Every send and every dollar stays the human's.

## Principles
- Captured demand and created demand are different machines. Someone
  searching your category is finishing a decision; someone who has never
  felt the pain is starting one. One conversion window flatters the
  first and buries the second.
- A landing page makes one promise and keeps it above the fold. Traffic
  that arrives expecting one thing and reads another converts like
  traffic that never arrived.
- Attribution is a convention, not a truth. Last-touch is a tie-break
  rule; multi-touch is a model with assumptions. Both are reportable,
  neither is a fact, and the assumptions go beside the number.
- Paid acquisition multiplies the conversion rate that already exists,
  including zero (`skills/marketing.md`). Spend prices a message; it
  does not create one.
- A nurture sequence has an exit. A list nobody ever graduates off is a
  treadmill being reported as engagement.

## Defaults
- Every campaign declares its target metric, its spend cap, and its kill
  date before the first send. Spend is a human decision
  (.Doctrine.md §5); this skill proposes budgets and buys nothing.
- Lifecycle email written as a finite sequence: one trigger, a fixed
  number of steps, an exit on conversion or on silence.
- One landing page per promise, one CTA per page, and a form asking only
  for what the next step actually needs.
- Consent, unsubscribe, and retention wired before the first send rather
  than retrofitted after growth; the duties are
  `skills/privacy-compliance.md`.
- Funnel reporting labels measured vs modeled per stage and cites its
  source (`skills/metrics.md`); one figure never appears twice under two
  definitions.

## Do
- Publish the funnel definition — what counts as a lead, a qualified
  lead, an activation — before reporting any rate against it.
- Instrument the whole path before buying traffic to it; unattributed
  spend is a donation with a dashboard.
- Hand off to `skills/sales.md` at a written threshold, with the source
  and the research attached, where a human takes it from there.
- Kill or scale on the declared metric at the declared date
  (`skills/marketing.md`'s channel-experiment rule, applied here), record
  what the result taught the next campaign.

## Don't
- Don't buy traffic to a page that has not yet converted warm traffic
  (`skills/marketing.md`).
- Don't report a blended CAC that hides a losing channel inside a
  winning one.
- Don't email anyone who did not consent, and never widen a list
  gathered for one purpose into another.
- Don't count a form fill as demand; a row is not an intent.
- Don't send, spend, or publish — every sequence, page, and ad is a
  draft queued for the human.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Funnel stages defined in writing before any rate is reported
- [ ] Spend cap, target metric, and kill date declared up front
- [ ] Landing page makes one promise, keeps it, carries one CTA
- [ ] Nurture sequence has a trigger, a step count, and an exit
- [ ] Attribution model named; its assumptions written beside the number
- [ ] Consent and unsubscribe verified; all sends queued for the human

## Common failure modes
Captured and created demand averaged into one meaningless rate; a
nurture sequence with no exit that trains its list to filter you;
last-touch read as causation until the channel that created the demand
gets cut; spend scaled past its kill date because some other number was
green; a list inherited with a consent basis nobody can source.
